# Represent and retrieve internals

The six views, region proposal, the frame-segment link, and how the tiers fuse into a ranked list with evidence.

> Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-08-15, verbatim. Every number here is a measurement
> taken from a real recording or a real run — not an estimate. Re-measure before changing one.

**CAPTIONING IS THE PIPELINE'S WHOLE BILL, AND THE BILL IS IMAGE TOKENS.** Measured over two real indexing runs (`index_job.progress`, 192.9m of stage time): `captions` 103.1m / **53.5%**, the since-retired `appCaptions` 74.3m / **38.5%**, `framePatches` 10.2m / 5.3%, the whole compose ladder 4.2m / **2.2%**, and the nine remaining stages 1.1m between them. So "combine the compose levels" saves nothing measurable and "send the model fewer pixels" is the only lever that moves the total.

Benchmarked warm on two real keyframes with the configured captioner (`muse-glimmer:30b-mlx`), one image per call:

| width | prompt tokens | frame A | frame B | read `1+2+3` off the calculator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2560 (as stored) | 4113 | 224.0s | 154.3s | yes |
| 1280 | 1389 | 91.4s | 107.3s | yes |
| 896 | 756 | 55.5s | 47.9s | yes |

**WHAT SHIPPED WAS MEASURED IN PRODUCTION, AND ONLY ON LATENCY.** Two recordings indexed under the cap (`captionMaxWidth = 1280`, `index_job.progress`, 2026-08-24) against the two that produced the table above:

| | old A | old B | new C | new D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| keyframes | 50 | 23 | 22 | 21 |
| `captions` | 71.1m (85.3s each) | 32.0m (83.4s) | **11.0m (34.7s)** | **10.6m (35.3s)** |
| `appCaptions` | 65.9m (84.2s) | 8.4m (23.0s) | *retired* | *retired* |
| whole job | 147.1m | 45.8m | **16.2m** | **15.7m** |

Per caption **84.4s → 35.0s, a 2.4x cut**, and that is the width alone — skipping the recorder does not make a caption faster. End to end the rate is 1.99–2.94 min per keyframe → **0.74 min**, which projects the 73-keyframe corpus behind the 192.9m figure to **≈54m** against a ≈50m plan target. These are new recordings of comparable work, not a re-index of identical input, so read the per-caption number as the strong one.

**The CONTENT axis is not settled, and one run made it look settled.** A 2560/1920/1280 sweep read 86.4s / 59.0s / 36.8s per frame — 1280 corroborating the 35.0s measured in production — and printed *"1280px reads 100% of the on-screen text the 2560px did — SAFE"* at every width. Its control had recovered **one** on-screen string out of ten, so that sentence compared 1 against 1; the run had also silently taken `--limit 10` because npm ate the `--widths` flag in front of its value. Both are now refused by the probe (see the `probe:caption` entry in `CLAUDE.md`). What actually supports 1280 today is latency plus a spot-read of the captions it produced, which recover `1+2+3`, `1+2+3+4+` and the window title `Untitled — Edited`. A powered content sweep is `npm run probe:caption -- --widths 2560,1920,1280 --limit 33`, and the corpus caps it there: only 40 action segments carry a quoted on-screen string at all.

Working backwards from the token counts the model sustains ~25 tok/s of prompt processing, so **image tokens are essentially the whole cost and capping generation with `num_predict` is a minor lever by comparison** — do not start there.

**`captionMaxWidth` IS A SECOND CAP AND MUST NOT BE FOLDED INTO `imageMaxWidth`.** The stored width belongs to the image model: ColModernVBERT's preprocessor upscales below 2048px and match quality degrades with no visible error, which is why `SettingsScreen` banners the user to raise it to 2560. Lowering what is STORED would buy the captioner its speed with the image model's accuracy, and with the player's and the patch highlighter's resolution too. So the resize happens on the way to the model and nothing is written back. It is an injected `ImageDownscaler` rather than a line inside `OllamaCaptionProvider` because that adapter is barrel-safe and `sharp` is native — the line is **native module, not subprocess** — and `sharpDownscaler` uses `withoutEnlargement`, so the cap is a ceiling and a keyframe captured narrower than it is never upscaled to meet it. **Absence passes the original bytes through**: a downscaler that fails to load must cost time, never a caption. `npm run probe:caption` decides the width against the digest's own strings.

**THE RECORDER IS EXCLUDED FROM CAPTIONING, NOT ONLY FROM THE TRACE LIFT.** Measured on the real store, **114 of 367 captions described the DeskRAG window** — 41 of 47 in one recording — at roughly 85s each. Worse than the time: `ComposeRepresenter.leafChildren` reads `s.caption ?? s.digest`, **caption first**, so recorder prose climbed the ladder and three of eight composed roots read "Record screen capture while taking notes", "monitor active session recording" and "record screen with DeskRAG" for sessions about a calculator, the news and Spotify. `captionExclusionFor` (`app/src/main/caption-exclusion.ts`, pure and root-suite reachable) composes the SAME `excludedByName` predicate and the same latest-at-or-before focus resolution `excludeFocusedApps` uses, and carries both of that function's hard-won rules: **no `focus_change` anywhere is a no-op** — with `active-win` off nothing is attributable and a walk that started excluded would caption nothing at all — and **the preamble belongs to the first `focus_change`**, because segmentation pins the first boundary at t_mono 0 where no focus event exists yet, and that opening stretch is exactly the recorder the Record button was clicked in. An excluded segment is skipped BEFORE its blob is read and keeps `caption = null`, composing from its digest.

Verified on the two recordings taken after the change: **0 of 37 captions mention the recorder** (118 of 367 did before, matching `DeskRAG` or `Recorder`), **0 name a device class**, and both composed roots name the work — *Calculate and document result*, *Taking notes and performing calculations* — where every earlier root named the recording. Parents restating a child verbatim went 3 of 27 to 0 of 6, which is **not** a measurement at six parents and is reported as consistent, not confirmed; one of the three old duplicates was the recorder line, the other two were not. The residue is expected and is not a leak: an excluded segment still reaches the ladder through its digest, so level-1 nodes still read *use Electron app*.

**`app_caption` IS RETIRED, AND THE CROP MADE IT WORSE RATHER THAN MERELY REDUNDANT.** It was a second VLM pass over the same keyframe, cropped to the focused window's bounds. Stripped of the menu bar, the Dock and the wallpaper, the model lost the cues that name the platform and reached for the interface it had seen most often: **73 of 363 (20.1%) called a macOS window an iOS, iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch screen**, against **1 of 367** whole-frame captions — and that one was legitimate (Chrome on Google News, correctly placed on macOS). The text was not inert: `indexSegmentText` concatenated it into the FTS row and `buildRetriever` gave it its own dense Tier-1 lane, duplicating the caption lane's vote at 0.37 Jaccard by vocabulary. Nothing else read it — not compose, not the trace lift, not habits, not the record. It is in `RETIRED_VIEWS`, so `DualStore` drops the Lance table on open. **`segment_app_caption` stays declared and stays in `DERIVED_SESSION_TABLES`**: every store predating the retirement holds those rows, they are now both unread and wrong, and staying classified is what makes `purgeDerived` empty them on the next re-index. Dropping the classification would strand them on disk permanently.

**Frame-level dedup must not key on pHash.** 153 of 359 consecutive keyframe pairs in the real store are Hamming distance 0 and 211 are within 2, so skipping "duplicate" frames looks nearly free. It would delete exactly the content that matters: the calculator frames are among the Hamming-0 pairs and differ by one digit — `1+2` against `1+2+3` — which a 64-bit DCT hash over an 8×8 low-frequency grid cannot see by construction, and which is the whole retrievable content of the segment. Any such dedup has to key on something that resolves small high-frequency change: the AX tree, the region labels, or the digest.

**The 6 views** (`VIEWS` in `src/embed/types.ts`, and still six after two rounds of retirement — the list has turned over, not grown): transcript (STT — `FfmpegAudioProducer` captures chunks, `TranscriptRepresenter` + `WhisperCppTranscription` turn them into text; a `FakeTranscription` keeps the tests pure), caption (VLM), digest (templated event text), summary (a COMPOSED level's text), behavior (12-dim input-dynamics vector, a "builtin" namespace — not a network provider), frame_patches (multi-vector late interaction). `RETIRED_VIEWS` holds the three that left: `frame_image` and `region_image` with the single-vector image lane, and `app_caption` for the reason above. Tier-1 fuses views with **Reciprocal Rank Fusion**, not score averaging (scales differ).

**A segment's span is NOT the span of the speech inside it, and `transcript_clip` is where that fact lives.** `TranscriptRepresenter` already computed per-utterance offsets from whisper's `-oj` output to slice text per segment, and then discarded them — so the only persisted record of speech was a segment span, and the rail drew a 10s bar for a two-second sentence, repeated across every segment the audio blob touched. Clips keep `blob.tMonoStart + s.startMs` as absolute `t_mono`. The segment-level `segment.transcript` column is unchanged: it is what the Tier-1 `transcript` view embeds, and clips are an additional finer record, never a replacement. A provider that reports no timestamps writes **no clips** rather than a guessed interval spanning the whole blob — and the rail's transcript lane then falls back to segment spans *and says so in `warning`*.

**Region proposal (`represent/regions/`, the PixelRAG edge):** fuse three sources — AX tree (`axFilter`: real labeled bboxes, filtered hard), interaction hotspots (weighted DBSCAN over clicks/dwell — the signal video RAG lacks), and grid tiling — via NMS with a cross-source agreement priority bump, then a budget cut. AX role/label is also written to SQLite FTS5 so regions are text-searchable by UI role.

**FRAME↔SEGMENT LINKS ARE WHAT MAKE TEXT SEARCH RETURN ANYTHING, and must never be gated on a provider.** A text query recalls frames purely by segment membership, so with no `frame_segment` rows it returns ZERO results over a full library — no error, no diagnostic. They were written only inside the image representers, gated on an image provider defaulting to `"none"`: measured, **2 of 4 real recordings and 38 of 86 frames were unreachable by any query.** `associateFrames` (`represent/frame-segments.ts`) is pure SQLite and runs as its own always-on stage; `FramePatchRepresenter` calls the same `segmentIdsForFrame` so the window rule cannot drift. **The suite could not see this — every `assemble.test.ts` case built the `Retriever` WITH an image embedder.** Test the DEFAULT configuration; it is the one nobody runs.

**The digest is a retrieval surface, not a tally.** When `action` stopped subdividing, segments went to **945ms** while `buildDigest` still summarized ~10s windows: over 105 real segments **86% were `1 click.` / `mouse movement.` / `idle segment`** — identical strings embedding to one vector with arbitrary ANN order. It now carries the window title, URL, typed text and the label of what was clicked (86% → 4%). Two orderings hold it up: **Regions runs BEFORE Digest** (the labels come from what that stage wrote), and **focus resolves latest-at-or-before**, since a 945ms segment rarely contains its own `focus_change`.

**Typed text is coalesced at SESSION scope, and `typedRuns` is deliberately NOT `groupGestures`.** Typing IS a visual state change, so per-segment grouping shredded a sentence into `typed "this is"` / `typed "a test"` / `typed "of the"` — the phrase existed in no segment. `groupGestures` cannot serve this: it flushes on ANY non-key event because a replayable `type` action must be contiguous. The grouping POLICY differs by purpose; the decoder (`resolveKeys`) stays shared. A run is carried WHOLE into every segment it overlaps, and **backspace is applied** (scancode 14, beside `SCANCODE_TO_VK`) so a run is what ended up on screen — without it a real recording read `"the mergeemergency braoadcast system"`.

**`DEFAULT_RRF_K` is 5, not the published 60, and reverting it inverts the ranking.** 60 suits RRF's original regime (few systems, top-1000 lists, millions of docs). Here FIVE lanes (digest, summary, caption, transcript, lexical) run over a corpus starting at a few hundred segments — **six when every sweep below was run, before `app_caption` was retired**, so the lane-COUNT term spans 6× while RANK over a 100-long list spans 2.6× — a segment ranked ~20th everywhere beats one ranked 1st in two lanes *by construction*. Measured: a typed sentence was #1 in the digest lane AND #1 in the lexical lane, and came **13th** fused. **It was 10 while there were five lanes; adding `summary` widened the count term and it was re-swept** — FOUR real recordings, 183 segments, 53 known-answer queries: mean rank 19.0/25.0/32.6/43.3 and recall@5 58%/28%/15%/6% at k=5/10/20/60, monotonic. Swept four times as the library grew and the margin WIDENS every time — recall@5 at k=5 vs k=10 went 47/33 → 52/27 → 58/28. `Tier1Retriever` also carried its own literal `60` — read the constant, never a literal. **`rrfK` reaches the fusion only as `RetrieverOptions.tier1.rrfK`**; passed at the top level it is silently ignored, which looks exactly like an inert sweep since every k then returns one ranking.

**RETIRING `app_caption` NARROWED THE COUNT TERM, AND `DEFAULT_RRF_K` HAS NOT BEEN RE-SWEPT SINCE.** The constant moved from 10 to 5 precisely *because* adding `summary` widened the lane count from five to six; dropping back to five is the same change in reverse, and the argument that set it does not say which way the optimum moves. It is left at 5 deliberately rather than adjusted by reasoning — the four previous sweeps all found the margin widening as the library grew, and a fifth against known answers is what would settle it. Treat 5 as unswept-at-five-lanes until then.

**`FrameResult.score` IS AN ORDERING, NOT A QUALITY MEASURE, AND ITS TOP VALUE IS A STRUCTURAL CONSTANT.** `assemble()` sums `w.frame·(frameScore/maxFrame) + w.region·(regionScore/maxRegion) + w.segment·(segmentScore/maxSegment)`, and **all three maxima are taken over the CURRENT candidate set**. So the best hit of every query lands at the weight ceiling however good or bad it is, and the number is not comparable between two queries. On the default install (`imageProvider: "none"`) every frame distance is `NaN`, `frameScore` is identically 0, and the score collapses to `0.5·region + 0.5·segment` — **1.000 for the top hit of every search, or a flat 0.500 when no AX region matched at all.** That is what the "score 1.000" recorded below and in `docs/mcp.md` actually is. A Tier-4 rerank also reorders without recomputing, so the list is not even guaranteed monotonic in it.
- **So the UI shows RANK and EVIDENCE, never the number.** `FrameEvidence` (required on `FrameResult`) keeps the three normalized terms — weight them and add and `score` comes back, so the breakdown can never disagree with the ranking — plus `lanes`, the ranked lists the hit appeared in, from `SegmentHit.perView` + `lexicalRank` + Tier 3's `ftsRank`. **Agreement across independent lanes is the only thing here that means "good"**: a frame three lists rank highly is trustworthy, one scraping in on `lexical` alone is a stretch. All of it was computed on every query and discarded before this.
- **An empty `lanes` is MEANINGFUL, not missing data.** The frame scope is expanded to leaves (`getDescendantLeaves`) before recall, so a frame pulled in under a composed parent hit genuinely appeared in no list of its own — "recalled with its segment", which is what the row says.
- The renderer names lanes for the reader, not for the index (`digest` → *what happened*, `region_label` → *on-screen label*), and an unknown key falls through to ITSELF rather than to a catch-all, so a lane added later looks unfinished instead of being silently absorbed.

**A COMPOSED LEVEL IS UNDER-RANKED BY CONSTRUCTION, AND NO `k` FIXES IT.** A `level:N`/`session` row has ONLY a summary — no digest, no caption, no transcript — so it participates in ONE dense lane where a leaf participates in two or three (three or four before `app_caption` was retired), and RRF is a SUM. Measured over 20 composed queries whose own `summary` lane ranked them **#1 every time**: none came first fused, ranks splitting 3,4,4,4,4,5,5,9,11 / 26,29,29,30,32,33,34,34,35,36. One in full — query `"Start calculator"`: the correct `level:1` had `summary#1`, the winning `action` had `digest#25 caption#32 app_caption#1 transcript#13` (that run predates `app_caption`'s retirement, which removed one of the four lanes the leaf was winning on). So "retrieval at altitude" works less well than the summary view promises: a task is findable but rarely first. Fixing it means participation-normalizing the score (which discards the cross-lane agreement RRF was chosen for) or ranking altitudes separately — both design changes, neither made. Leaf retrieval is unaffected (mean rank 1.40 at k=5).

**Tier 1 has a lexical lane; Tier 3's text half needs no model.** `segment_fts` holds each segment's digest+caption+transcript+**summary**, written by ONE always-on stage at the END of indexing so it sees whatever the provider-gated stages actually produced. A composed level carries no digest or caption of its own — the summary IS its text, so without it a task is unreachable by an exact term. It needs no provider, so on a default install it is the only route from a query to an EXACT term — where dense views are weakest. Tier 3 likewise takes an OPTIONAL `imageEmbedder`: its AX-label half is the only per-frame evidence a text query has, and without it every frame sharing a segment scores identically (measured: 11 frames tied to six decimals). Region FTS PRE-filters by frame scope like the ANN half — post-filtering a global top-N returns nothing once the library exceeds the limit. FTS ranks are DENSE and keyed on role+label, so frames showing the same control tie rather than being ordered by bm25's arbitrary tie-break.

**`putSegmentVectors` is a bare `lance.add`.** Re-running a represent stage writes a SECOND vector under one id, and neither is an orphan, so `reconcile()` can never prune it — verified: 8 vectors for 4 segments. Any path that may run twice uses `replaceSegmentVectors`; that is what made re-indexing safe. **The FTS tables have no foreign key**, so `deleteSession` clears `region_fts` and `segment_fts` explicitly or a deleted recording keeps answering searches.

- **WHISPER'S BRACKETED ANNOTATIONS ARE DROPPED AT WRITE TIME, and the rule is TEXT, not energy.** whisper.cpp brackets whatever it could not hear as speech — `(soft music)`, `[BLANK_AUDIO]`, `*sighs*` — and downstream nothing can tell those from words: they reach `transcript_clip`, `segment.transcript`, the transcript VECTOR SPACE and the digest corpus, making a segment look like it held speech while adding nothing anyone would search for. Measured on the real store rather than assumed: of **24 clips, 22 were annotations and 0 were plausible speech**, and 22 segments carried `"(soft music) (dramatic music)"` as their searchable text once computer audio was recording too — a desktop is a music-and-silence corpus far more often than a spoken one. The filter is deliberately a text rule (`isNonSpeechText`), applied to the clips, to the per-segment string, and to the no-timestamps fallback: an ENERGY GATE would need a floor nobody has swept, and set a shade too high it silently drops quiet speech, which is the failure this pipeline is least able to notice. **What no text rule can catch is a hallucinated WORD** — the same measurement found `"You"` transcribed from a chunk that was digitally silent at −91 dB, and that is indistinguishable from someone saying "you".
